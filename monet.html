03 — MONET · 1840–1926

Claude Monet

A grocer's son from Le Havre who painted the same light for sixty years, lost almost everything twice, and ended up building his own garden to paint.

1840 Paris

  1. Born in Paris

    14 NOVEMBER 1840

    Oscar-Claude Monet is born in Paris. At about five he moves with his family to Le Havre, on the Normandy coast, where the sky and the sea first become his subject.

  2. Learning to paint outdoors

    1858

    He sells caricatures around town until the painter Eugène Boudin takes him to the shore and teaches him to work from nature, not from the studio.

  3. Paris and new friends

    1859–1865

    He studies in Paris and, after a spell in military service, meets Renoir, Sisley and Bazille. Together they begin painting modern life in fast, bright strokes.

  4. The name Impressionism

    1874

    His harbour scene Impression, Sunrise hangs in the first group exhibition. A critic mocks the title, and the movement takes its name from the insult.

  5. Loss and the Seine

    1879

    His first wife, Camille, dies at 32. Money is tight, but he keeps painting the river and its winter ice at Vétheuil.

  6. A house at Giverny

    1883

    He rents a pink house with a long garden in a village west of Paris. He stays for the rest of his life.

  7. One subject, many hours

    1890–1894

    The Haystacks, then Rouen Cathedral. He works on many canvases at once, switching as the light shifts, to paint the weather and not the object.

  8. The water garden

    1893–1899

    He buys the land across the road, diverts a stream and builds a lily pond with a Japanese footbridge. It becomes his main subject for the next 25 years.

  9. Grief, then the great panels

    1911–1918

    His second wife, Alice, dies in 1911 and his son Jean in 1914. He answers with huge Water Lilies canvases, and after the 1918 armistice offers them to France.

  10. Failing sight

    1912–1923

    Cataracts blur and yellow his vision. He keeps painting, then has surgery in 1923, and the cooler blues return to his palette.

  11. The last winter

    5 DECEMBER 1926

    Monet dies at Giverny, aged 86. The Water Lilies open to the public the next year at the Musée de l'Orangerie in Paris.
